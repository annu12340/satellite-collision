Chapter 1 — orbital_mechanics.py

A secret nobody tells you about orbit

I'm not "flying."

I'm falling — sideways, fast enough that I keep missing the ground, forever. That's the whole trick.

Meanwhile Earth's lumpy equator keeps tugging at me sideways, sunlight leans on me like a slow-motion bully, and thin wisps of leftover atmosphere quietly sand down my orbit.

My operators are never quite sure exactly where I am, either.

Comforting.

"Falling with style." — me, misquoting a movie about a toy astronaut.

Chapter 2 — conjunction.py

The one who watches my back

I don't have eyes. I can't see the other 10,000+ tracked objects sharing this orbit with me.

What I do have is a very patient algorithm, quietly ruling out ~50 million boring pairings in microseconds, then running the real math — the actual probability-of-collision integral, the one real operators trust — on the handful that matter.

Cross 1-in-10,000 odds and it doesn't panic.

It just gets to work.

03
Chapter 3 — avoidance.py

The world's smallest emergency brake

I don't have brakes. I don't have a steering wheel.

I have about 25 m/s of fuel for my entire operational life — a shot glass, not a gas tank.

So when a burn gets called, it's not dramatic. It's a whisper, days ahead of time, and I let orbital mechanics do the dramatic part for me.

A tiny nudge now beats a desperate shove later. Every single time.

04
Chapter 4 — damage_minimization.py

If I can't dodge, I at least get to choose

Sometimes the warning comes too late. Sometimes the tank's already empty.

That's when the question stops being "can we avoid this" and becomes "how do we make this suck less."

Turn edge-on instead of flat-on. Aim for a glancing sideswipe instead of a bullseye.

Bias the wreckage toward an altitude where the atmosphere cleans it up in months, not centuries.

It's the closest thing I get to planning my own funeral.

Chapter 5 — risk_optimizer.py

Turns out I'm one of many problems

Humbling discovery: I'm not the only satellite having a bad day.

There are hundreds of us, all sharing the same finite fuel and attention, all tangled together — dodge one thing and you might steer straight into another.

So the system stops thinking about just me and starts treating the whole constellation as one risk network, hunting for the single move that defuses an entire cluster at once.

It borrows the algorithm that beat humans at Go.

I try not to take it personally that I'm currently a chess piece.
